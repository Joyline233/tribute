About Her

Saint Teresa of Calcutta (1910 – 1997)

Mother Teresa, born Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu on August 26, 1910, in Skopje, North Macedonia, was a Roman Catholic nun and missionary who devoted her entire life to serving the poorest of the poor. At the age of 18, she left her home to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland, and later moved to India, where she taught for many years before answering what she described as "a call within a call" — a divine instruction to serve the dying and destitute on the streets of Calcutta (Kolkata).


In 1950, she founded the Missionaries of Charity, a congregation that grew from 12 members to over 4,000 sisters running homes for people with HIV/AIDS, leprosy, and tuberculosis, as well as orphanages, schools, and soup kitchens around the world. Her selfless service earned her the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979, and she was canonized as a saint by the Catholic Church in 2016.

Life Timeline

  • 1910 — Born Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu in Skopje, Ottoman Empire (now North Macedonia).
  • 1928 — Left home at 18 to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland.
  • 1929 — Arrived in India, where she began her novitiate in Darjeeling.
  • 1937 — Took her final vows and became "Mother Teresa".
  • 1948 — Answered the "call within a call" and began working among the poor of Calcutta.
  • 1950 — Founded the Missionaries of Charity.
  • 1952 — Opened the first Home for the Dying (Nirmal Hriday) in Calcutta.
  • 1979 — Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.
  • 1997 — Passed away on September 5 in Calcutta, India.
  • 2016 — Canonized as Saint Teresa of Calcutta by Pope Francis.

In Her Own Words

"If you can't feed a hundred people, then feed just one."
"Spread love everywhere you go. Let no one ever come to you without leaving happier."
"Not all of us can do great things. But we can do small things with great love."

Her Legacy

Mother Teresa showed the world that true greatness lies in humility and service. Her life reminds us that compassion needs no wealth, no power, and no fame — only a willing heart. Today, the Missionaries of Charity continues her work in over 130 countries, caring for the forgotten and the abandoned.


She taught us timeless values:

  • Compassion — See the dignity in every human being.
  • Humility — Serve quietly, without seeking recognition.
  • Perseverance — Keep giving even when the need feels endless.
  • Love in action — Kindness is worth more than words.

She is the person I love most in history because she proved that one person's love, practiced daily in small ways, can change the world.
